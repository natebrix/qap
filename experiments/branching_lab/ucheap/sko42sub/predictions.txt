What hyb.par did: a -F run's start node has no inherited bound, so its relative gap is large and pick_param puts it
in block 0 whatever its depth (the trace's rule level is 0 at the start node in every arm of every batch). Block 0
of hyb.par is rule 9, with branch.xbar_lines = 2. So the depth-2 node got rule 9 with two lines a side, not rule 10,
and the nodes below got rule 11 with two lines (block 3, as meant).
- 36:14,7:0: the depth-2 line is c35 in all three arms, so this is a clean reading of the rule from depth 3 down
  over the same seven depth-3 subtrees: rule 9 5,014 s, rule 10 5,754, rule 11 with two lines 5,924 (8 branchings
  at depth 4 for 3 and 5). "r11x2 was never behind rule 9 from depth 3" does not hold here; the hybrid's reason
  is gone. A hybrid with rule 10 at the depth-2 node would have run exactly this (rule 10 takes c35 too).
- 36:15,27:0: rule 9 with two lines a side took r22: 882 nodes, against c41's 691 (one line a side) and c35's 235
  (rule 10). Not a test of the hybrid; one more reading that the depth-2 line is where the factor is.

0's 25 lookahead iterations; in the full run a depth-2 node is in block 1, 12 iterations, or block 2, 25.)
